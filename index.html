You're good at the work. The business underneath it is the problem.

Every month starts at zero. You quote, you chase, you shoot, you invoice, you wait. A good month means you were busy, not that you were safe. A quiet month means the mortgage comes out of savings.

The standard advice is to post more, grow your reach and build a better portfolio. That's a lottery ticket. It rewards the 23-year-old shooting cheap for Instagram clout, not the operator with 10 years of experience and a family.

Experienced operators win on reliability and communication with professional businesses. The problem is you've never packaged that into something a business can pay for every month.

Roughly 90% of my media company's revenue is retainers billed on the 1st of every month. I know what January looks like in October.

Retainers are the floor. One-off work is the ceiling.

Most creatives only have a ceiling. In 90 days you'll have monthly retainers covering your fixed costs, so every job after that is profit and choice.

  1. Calculate your floor. Your non-negotiable monthly fixed costs, business and personal minimum. That number is the target. We do it live on your kickoff call.
  2. Reverse engineer the retainers. How many, at what price, using my pricing and structuring method. Retainers sit at roughly two-thirds of your one-off rate, because reliability is worth more than margin.
  3. Mine your existing and past clients. Your first retainers are already in your inbox. We package the offer and pitch it. You'll be pitching within 2 weeks.
  4. Stack and systemise. Lock in the retainers, build a weekly schedule around them, tighten the workflow so they take less time each month.
  5. Sell the ceiling. Remaining capacity goes to one-off work at 2 to 3 times your retainer rate. You take it because you want to, not because you have to.

Retainer clients are always businesses, never consumers. A wedding photographer's retainer clients are the venue, the planner and the florist, not the couple.

Questions

I don't have many clients yet. Will this work?

No. The method converts clients you already have or have worked with. If you're still building a client base, follow the free content and apply when you've got a few paying clients behind you.

I shoot weddings. Do I have retainer clients?

Yes, but they're not couples. They're the venues, planners, florists and celebrants who need content every month. If there are none of those in your market, this isn't for you yet.

How much time does it take each week?

Plan for 3 to 5 hours a week in the first month, mostly pitching and follow-up. Less after retainers are signed.

What if a client says no?

Most will. The target is 10 pitches minimum because that's what it takes to land 3 to 5. We work on the no's together.

Will retainers lower my rates?

Per deliverable, usually yes, by about a third. Per month, you earn more because the work is guaranteed and your one-off rate goes up once you're not desperate for it.
